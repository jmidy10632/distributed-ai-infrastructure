# Contributing

Thank you for taking the time to look at this work.

> Teamwork makes the dream work.

This project is too broad for any one person or discipline to solve alone. It will become stronger only if people bring different expertise, experiences, regions, and disagreements to the same table with honesty and respect.

I am not asking anyone to agree with the paper before taking part. A correction, counterexample, failed experiment, or better explanation may be more valuable than support. If something in the paper is wrong, overstated, or impractical, I want to know.

Please keep contributions politically neutral and focused on the evidence or design. The project-wide statement is in [POLITICAL_NEUTRALITY.md](POLITICAL_NEUTRALITY.md).

The project is also geographically neutral. Please state where evidence comes from and avoid treating one country's grid, laws, costs, climate, or public process as universal. The full statement is in [GEOGRAPHIC_NEUTRALITY.md](GEOGRAPHIC_NEUTRALITY.md).

## Ways to take part

Start a Discussion when an idea is still broad or when you want to question the direction of the paper. Open an Issue for a specific factual problem, missing source, technical risk, or documented project outcome. A pull request is welcome when you have a concrete change to the text, references, measurements, or design.

There is no required position. It is entirely reasonable to conclude that a workload belongs in a modern data center, that a distributed approach is less efficient, or that a proposed safeguard is inadequate.

## What makes a useful contribution

The most helpful comments identify the passage or assumption at issue, explain the concern in plain language, and show the evidence or reasoning behind it. It also helps to say what information would change your own view.

Original records are preferred when they are available: permits, filings, election records, utility documents, datasets, standards, and the actual research paper rather than a summary of it. Good institutional research and careful reporting are also useful. Personal experience can add important context, but it should be identified as experience rather than treated as a verified general result.

Please keep facts, forecasts, assumptions, proposals, and personal judgments distinct. A source can be useful without proving every claim made from it.

## A few boundaries

- Discuss the work, not the character or motives of another person.
- Represent evidence against your position fairly.
- Disclose a financial, professional, organizational, or advocacy relationship when it could reasonably matter.
- Do not post confidential, proprietary, personal, export-controlled, or unlawfully obtained material.
- Do not treat technical efficiency alone as proof of a lower total environmental impact.
- Do not turn project discussions into party or candidate advocacy.
- Identify the geographic scope of a claim and avoid generalizing a local result to the world without support.

## What happens to a contribution

I may accept a change, revise it with the contributor, leave it open while more evidence is gathered, or explain why I do not think it belongs in the paper. A rejected edit does not make the underlying criticism disappear. Good-faith objections should remain visible so later readers can judge them for themselves.

John D. Midyette is the initial author and editor. As the project grows, contributors who do substantial research, analysis, writing, or review will be credited with their consent. Merging a small edit does not automatically make someone a coauthor, and being credited does not mean endorsing every part of the paper.

Contributed prose and documentation will be shared under the repository's Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license. Any future code or dataset will need its own clear and compatible license.
