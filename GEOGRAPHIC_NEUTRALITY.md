# A Note on Geographic Neutrality

This project concerns a global issue, and no country or region is the default.

The first draft includes several sources and project examples from the United States. They are included because they help illustrate particular grid, water, permitting, and community questions. They should not be read as a complete picture of data-center development or decentralized computing around the world.

The same architecture may have very different consequences in different places. Energy sources, grid reliability, climate, water availability, network quality, device ownership, hardware costs, labor conditions, laws, institutions, languages, and community priorities all matter. A result from one location should remain attached to that location unless there is evidence that it applies more broadly.

Contributions from every region are welcome. Local knowledge should be treated as substantive evidence, not as an exception to a supposedly universal model. Comparisons should explain their geographic boundaries and should avoid ranking countries or communities by assumptions that have not been examined.

Geographic neutrality does not mean geographic differences are ignored. It means those differences are described accurately and evaluated on their own terms.
