# Data-Center Project Outcomes

Public discussion often reduces data-center projects to a national trend or a political scorecard. I would rather build a record that can be checked one project at a time.

The registry is global. No country or region is the baseline, and entries should preserve the local legal, infrastructure, environmental, economic, and community context needed to understand an outcome.

An entry belongs here when it identifies a specific project, place, outcome, and date and points to an official or otherwise authoritative record. Reporting can add context, but an applicant's withdrawal, planning decision, ballot record, permit, court order, or utility filing is better evidence of what actually happened.

Possible outcomes include approval, approval with conditions, delay, denial, withdrawal, relocation, redesign, a moratorium, a ballot decision, construction, operation, or cancellation for a reason unrelated to public opposition. It is also acceptable to say that the outcome or its cause is unclear.

## Information worth recording

| Item | What it tells us |
| --- | --- |
| Project and location | Which proposal is being discussed, including country |
| Proponent | Who submitted or developed it |
| Proposed capacity | The scale of the project, with units and a source |
| Outcome and date | What happened and when |
| Decision authority | Who made or recorded the decision |
| Primary source | The filing, minutes, ballot record, order, permit, or withdrawal |
| Contributing factors | Reasons actually supported by the record |
| Public opposition | What occurred and how it is documented |
| Confidence | Whether the account is confirmed, probable, disputed, or incomplete |
| Later developments | Redesigns, new applications, litigation, or a changed outcome |

## Current state

The examples in the working paper should not be treated as verified entries merely because they are cited. They still need to be checked against primary records and updated for later developments.

## Sharing an outcome

Please include the project, location, proponent, proposed capacity, outcome, decision date, decision authority, primary source, other useful sources, documented contributing factors, public-opposition activity, later developments, and any uncertainty.

The relationship between opposition and an outcome should not be assumed. If the record supports several causes, all of them should remain visible.

Over time, the registry may reveal a pattern. Any count based on it will still need to state the geography, time period, search method, inclusion rules, and likely gaps in coverage.
