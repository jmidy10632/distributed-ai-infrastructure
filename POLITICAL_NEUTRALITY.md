# A Note on Political Neutrality

I want to be unambiguous about this: the project is politically neutral.

It does not endorse or oppose a party, candidate, campaign, or ideology. A person's political affiliation is not evidence for or against a technical claim, and participation here does not imply a political position.

Public decisions still matter. Elections, ballot measures, permits, moratoria, court decisions, utility actions, project withdrawals, and organized opposition can tell us something about whether an infrastructure proposal is practical or publicly acceptable. When those events are discussed here, they should be tied to a specific place, date, decision, and source.

I do not want the project to describe an industry, political party, or community as having won or lost. That language may be quoted when it is relevant and clearly attributed, but it will not become the project's conclusion.

The standard is the same in every direction. Evidence supporting decentralized infrastructure receives no special treatment, and evidence supporting centralized infrastructure is not discounted. Regulation, ownership, financing, and public policy can be discussed when they affect the engineering question, but the discussion should remain about the work rather than partisan advocacy.

Project channels are not a place for campaign activity, fundraising, party messaging, or attacks based on political identity. If a useful factual contribution arrives wrapped in partisan language, the goal will be to preserve the evidence and remove the political framing.
